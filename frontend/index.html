Segment Inventory Audit

The same audience often gets a different name on every platform — "Fitness Enthusiasts" on one, "Fitness Freaks" on another. This view groups those look-alike segments back together, so you can see how many differently-named versions of the same audience you're effectively paying for.

Performance & Behaviour

Two questions, told as one story. First: how does each audience actually perform, platform by platform? Then: when two platforms both matched a segment under the same canonical name, did they match audiences that actually behave alike — or just audiences that happen to share a label?

Part 1 — How each audience performs

100 means an audience performs exactly like that platform's own average — above 100 means it outperforms, below means it underperforms. Because platforms measure things differently, every cell also carries a trust grade (A/B/C) for how safe it is to compare that number against another platform's.

A Safe to compare directly B Compare direction only C Treat as a hint, not a fact

Part 2 — Does the name match the behaviour?

Matching segments by name is easy — two platforms both calling something "fitness" doesn't mean they mean the same people. Below, every name-matched group is checked against how its members actually click and convert. Groups that split across more than one behaviour pattern are listed first — those are the ones worth a human review before you treat them as a single audience.

Category Affinity

Two questions about CPG categories, kept apart because they rest on different evidence. First: who over-buys energy drinks — audience by audience, indexed against each platform's own average. Second: what else ends up in the basket alongside them.

Target Groups & Next Best Action

Everything the other screens found, combined into one call per audience: what to do about it. Every number here traces back to a specific screen — hover the ⓘ marks to see exactly what's behind each one.
